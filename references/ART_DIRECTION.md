# Canopy Vanguard — Art Direction

## Elven archer reference

The user-supplied image at `references/wood-elf-archer.webp` is an inspiration reference for future original model rendering. It is not a production texture or a model sheet to reproduce literally.

Carry forward:

- A light, practical skirmisher silhouette rather than heavy heroic armor.
- Layered leaf-like plates, fitted bracers, tall boots, and an asymmetric cloak.
- Muted moss, olive, teal, bark-brown, and aged bronze.
- An organic bow with pale, branch-like limbs.
- Graceful, alert posture and equipment that appears grown, carved, or hand-worked.

Avoid copying the exact costume, pose, facial design, or ornament. New models should remain original.

## Elven village language

The village is a hidden refuge that belongs to the forest instead of replacing it. Buildings should appear coaxed around roots and trunks, linked by garden paths and branch-like arches.

- Use Art Nouveau “whiplash” curves, stems, petals, seed pods, and repeated botanical rhythms.
- Emphasize handcraft: carved timber, pale stone, aged copper, hammered fittings, and warm glass.
- Keep the settlement low, sheltered, and partially concealed by the canopy; reserve height and brighter light for communal landmarks.
- Shape doors and windows as tapered leaves, buds, and intersecting branches rather than rectangles.
- Use asymmetry in individual details while keeping larger spaces calm and legible.
- Palette: pale limestone, warm ivory, walnut, moss, deep teal, verdigris, amber lantern light.
- The player home should feel intimate and identifiable: a leaf-shaped threshold beneath a luminous copper-green arch.

## Reference synthesis

- [The Met — Art Nouveau](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/art-nouveau): botanical study, sinuous curves, and Arts and Crafts handwork.
- [Weta Workshop — Rivendell bridge](https://www.wetanz.com/uk/aragorn-and-arwen-mini): architecture presented in visual harmony with its landscape.
- [Tolkien Gateway — Rivendell](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Rivendell): hidden valley, sanctuary, and the “Last Homely House” as tonal—not literal—reference points.
- [D&D Beyond — Elf ancestry](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/basic-rules-2014/races): forest identity, seclusion, and a long-lived culture.
- [D&D Beyond — Feywild factions](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1021-feywild-101-factions-of-the-feywild-from-the): ancient magic and spectacular Elven settlements.
- [D&D Beyond — Dalelands art](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2101-art-of-the-forgotten-realms-the-dalelands): twisting trees and ancient Elven architecture as environmental motifs.

These references establish mood and design principles only. Production assets must use original shapes, layouts, motifs, and ornament.
